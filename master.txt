Executive Summary, Compact Timing and What Changed\nDariabandha (দাড়িয়াবান্ধা) is a traditional Bangladeshi line-guard game: one team guards lanes marked on the ground, the other tries to run through every lane to the back and return without being touched. This plan builds it as a 3D game that runs in a web browser on PC and Android, with one human per player and AI for every other seat.\n1.1 What changed in v2.0\nEvery decision is made. The 37 tick-boxes of v1.1 are now locked choices in Section 2, each with a reason.\nTechnology is chosen and justified: a web 3D game (Three.js) with a renderer-free rules engine, because it runs on PC and Android from one code base and can be built, tested and shipped without installing anything (Section 15).\nThe long match is compacted (below).\nA full technical plan is added: architecture, data, simulation, AI, rendering, audio, tests, build order, content list, constants and release (Sections 22–26).\n1.2 Compact timing (final) [[LOCKED]]\nThe reported 2×25 minute competitive match is far too long for digital play. Matches are compacted; clocks stop during transitions (capped at 6 s) so the shown times are real play time.\n2. Locked Decisions (All Made)\nEvery question from v1.1 is answered here. These are final unless changed through Appendix D.\n3. Vision, Goals, Non-Goals and Pillars\nOne-sentence pitch. A faithful, lane-based Dariabandha that brings a Bangladeshi playground game into a polished 3D experience where you control one athlete, and the whole team and court come alive around you.\nGoals\nPreserve the game’s identity: lanes, Sikka, the run through and back, and the tension of a single touch.\nTwo rule profiles that never mix consequences: Traditional and Competitive Standard (plus Quick Match).\nYou play one player, with AI teammates and opponents good enough that single-player is a real game.\nLegible rules: every decision (tag, foul, score) shows a reason.\nStable 60 FPS on agreed target hardware, with fair, identical outcomes at any frame rate.\nNon-goals for the first approved build\nNo pay-to-win, no stat-affecting cosmetics, no map-specific competitive advantages.\nNo uncontrolled physics deciding matches.\nNo unrelated sports, weapons, RPG stats or survival systems.\nNo advanced abilities inside Traditional or Competitive Standard (they belong to the Pro ruleset only).\nNo online ranked play at launch (phased, see D30).\nDesign pillars\n4. The table separates what is stated from what I inferred.\n5. Control Model: One Human Controls One Player\n\n5.1 Seats\nEach team has N numbered seats (2, 4 or 6). A seat is a persistent player identity for the whole match: same body, kit, name tag and stats.\nSeats change role by phase: when the team attacks, seats are attackers; when it defends, seats take lane slots (line guards and one Sikka). A human’s player simply changes role with the daan; the human never "picks a different player".\nIn the lobby the human states a preferred seat and role preference, including a Sikka request  LOCKED  D1. AI fills every empty seat and adopts the lane assignment the team captain makes.\nThe captain is the first human seat on a team (or the AI coordinator if the team has no human). The captain chooses the Sikka and lane assignments between rounds, when the rules allow. A human who does not want the job can hand it to the AI coordinator in the lobby.\n5.2 Who plays in each format\nBecause most 6v6 players on court will be AI, AI quality is a gating feature for the whole game (Section 12), not polish.\n5.3 Elimination, absence and disconnects\nAn eliminated or tagged-out human sees a spectate camera following teammates, with a visible "returns at…" label, so they always know when they re-enter  LOCKED  D2.\nIf a human disconnects or leaves, AI takes that seat within one daan transition; the human can rejoin and retake it at the next transition  LOCKED  D3.\nNo player-to-player "command" system exists in Traditional or Competitive Standard. In the Pro ruleset, a captain may send non-binding pings (Section 17).\nLocal couch co-op is out of v1 because one human per player means several humans sharing a camera  LOCKED  D4.\n5.4 Consequences for design\nAttackers act simultaneously: a human attacker is one runner among several; a team plan is expressed by timing and body language, not commands.\nA human guard is lane-bound and has a different control scheme from an attacker (Section 11).\nEvery HUD element is personal: "your role", "your lane", "you return in…".\nMatchmaking and ranked queues must count humans per seat; AI never appears in ranked queues  LOCKED  D31.\n6. Court Geometry, Lanes and Boundaries\n7.1 Units and reference court\nAll internal measurements are in metres; feet appear only to quote sources. The reference court is 50 × 20 ft = 15.24 × 6.10 m, five rows of 10 ft (3.05 m), each row split by the spine into two 10 × 10 ft squares.  PROPOSED  D17\n\nFigure 1. Candidate 6v6 court (Layout A). Guard positions are a placeholder until D18 is settled.\n7.2 Scaling to 2v2 and 4v4\nv1.0 shrank the court proportionally (24 × 12 ft and 36 × 16 ft). That would shrink boxes and break the balance between tag reach and box width. v1.1 keeps the box at 10 × 10 ft and removes rows instead.  PROPOSED  D19\n\nFigure 2. Same box size in every format.\nCourt width and lane layout for 2v2 and 4v4 are tuning parameters; they must be tested for attacker success rate (Section 12.5), not just visual appeal.\n7.3 Lanes and legal regions\n7.4 Boundary rule\nA line is a zone: the line’s visual half-width around its centreline (default 0.05 m each side).\nA guard’s position is two foot circles (radius 0.07 m). If any foot circle overlaps a boundary line zone of their own lane, they are "on the line"  LOCKED  D21.\nTeammates (guards and Sikka) do not block each other in lanes; where lanes intersect they may overlap, so no one can be trapped by a teammate.\nCourt lines are gameplay data first and art second. All maps use identical line data for a given format.\n8. Anything else lives in the Pro ruleset (Section 17).\n9. Match Flow, Tagging, Scoring and Restarts\n\nFigure 3. Match state machine (rules engine, no animation dependencies).\n9.1 Simulation and timing\nFixed simulation tick: 60 Hz (16.67 ms); rendering interpolates. Online server tick starts at 30 Hz with 60 Hz client simulation (Section 14).  PROPOSED \nAll rule windows are defined in ticks (for example the 0.50 s hold = 30 ticks) so results are identical at 30, 60 and 120 FPS.\nSame inputs and starting state must produce the same rule outcome; the event log records every call with a reason code.\n9.2 Valid tag algorithm (no code)\nEligibility: match is live; attacker is on court and not already tagged; guard is not in recovery from a missed tag.\nPosition: the guard’s foot circles are inside their own lane (not on the line zone) at the tick of contact.\nContact: the guard’s hand reach segment overlaps the attacker’s capsule during the tag’s active window.\nHold: the guard stays legally inside their lane for the hold window; a line step in the window voids the tag (call: "TAG VOID: FOOT ON LINE").\nResolution: first valid contact in tick order wins; ties at the same tick are broken by smaller reach distance, then lower seat number (deterministic, logged).\n9.3 Event priority\nPause or match-state check → legal-position validation → tag eligibility → contact resolution → hold validation → scoring or turn consequence → presentation.  PROPOSED  Animation, camera and UI never decide an outcome.\n9.4 Fouls and edge cases\n9.5 Transitions and scoring\nTransitions freeze input, show the reason ("TAGGED", "POINT", "FOOT ON LINE", "TIME"), reset only required positions, and cap at 6 s with the match clock stopped (D13).\nScoring table: Traditional = point per round trip (T4); Competitive/Quick = game point per round trip (C6). Tag consequences differ by profile (T5 vs C4/C5) and are never mixed.\n10. Movement, Stamina and Game Feel (First-Pass Numbers)\nMovement is natural but rule-bound: the legal region decides where you may go; the controller decides how you get there. Animation never changes speed. All values are  PROPOSED  starting points for the grey-box prototype (D37) and must be tuned by playtests.\n10.1 Stamina (attackers only)\n100 units. Sprint costs 18 per second; recovery is 10 per second while jogging and 16 per second standing. At 0 the attacker cannot sprint until stamina reaches 25; run speed never drops. No effect on turning, tagging, collision or reach.  LOCKED  D23\n10.2 Rules for feel\nDiagonal input is never faster than cardinal.\nAt lane edges, movement slides along the legal edge without jitter; a sprint cannot cross a restricted line.\nSurface type changes sound and visuals only, never speed, stopping distance or reach in any ranked mode.\nShort hit-stop (about 60 ms) on a valid tag is allowed for impact; it does not change the simulation.\nSanity check: crossing a 0.60 m lane takes a sprinting attacker about 0.19 s (1.2 m = lane plus capsule diameter, ÷ 6.4 m/s), shorter than tag wind-up plus active time (0.25 s). So an attacker who reads the guard’s position can beat a mistimed tag, which is the intended tension.\n11. Controls and Camera\n\n11.1 Role-specific control schemes\nAttacker: free movement relative to the camera; sprint; no tag. Pro adds feint and burst cut (Section 17).\nLine guard / Sikka: any stick or key direction is projected onto the lane axis, so left/right on screen is always along the lane; tag button; no sprint. Lane-aligned auto camera  LOCKED  D25.\nSpectate (eliminated): cycles through teammates; no gameplay input.\n11.2 Defaults\nTag has a 25° aim-assist cone that only helps choose a target already within reach; it never extends reach  LOCKED  D24.\nMobile layouts: left-handed mode, adjustable opacity and size, controls never cover lane intersections.\n11.3 Camera\nThird-person only  CONFIRMED . Default attacker camera: 5.0 m behind, 2.8 m high, pitch about −22°, field of view 70° (ranked uses a fixed field of view and one maximum zoom).  PROPOSED \nGuard camera looks from behind the guard toward the attackers’ approach, auto-aligned to the lane with at most ±25° yaw adjustment.\nSmall courts (2v2, 4v4) use per-format camera presets so the court stays readable.\nCamera never changes collision, reach or legal region, and never reveals hidden information. No heavy shake or motion blur; a reduced-motion option exists.\n12. AI Specification\nWith one human per player, AI controls most seats in single-player and fills any empty seat online. AI uses the same movement, lanes, stamina, tag reach and rules as humans. It never cheats on speed or reach; difficulty comes only from perception, timing and decision quality.\n12.1 Layers\n12.2 Difficulty tiers (set per team) [[LOCKED]] D5\n12.3 Fairness and quality rules\nNo rubber-banding or hidden bonuses; no ranking against AI (D31).\nAI decisions are seeded and loggable so tests are repeatable.\nAI never acts in transition states; AI takes over disconnected humans within one transition.\nAI must keep guards off boundary lines except by designed mistakes on lower tiers.\n12.4 Teammate behaviour you can feel\nAI teammates attack and defend in varied, believable patterns, not a visible script.\nWhen you are an attacker, AI teammates run decoys and staggered entries so your run feels like part of a plan.\nWhen you are a guard, the Sikka AI covers the spine according to the attackers’ positions.\n12.5 Balance targets [[PROPOSED]]\nAt equal tier, the share of daans where an attacker completes a round trip should land between 30% and 45% in every format and map. Technical Implementation Plan\n22.1 Repository layout\n22.2 Simulation loop\nFixed 60 Hz accumulator; at most 5 steps per frame; rendering interpolates between the last two states.\nSystem order each tick: match-state gate → collect inputs (human, AI, replay) → movement → collision separation → tag windows and hold → boundary checks → rules engine events → scoring and phase changes → event log.\nInput is a small struct per player per tick: move vector, sprint, tag, ping, feint, cut, lunge. Seeded RNG (mulberry32) is used only where randomness is designed (AI mistakes, toss).\nState hashing: a hash of all gameplay state every 60 ticks supports determinism tests and later network checks.\n22.3 Data models (fields)\n22.4 Movement and collision\nKinematic movement (no physics engine): velocity approaches target velocity with the acceleration and stop times in Section 25; diagonal speed is normalised.\nGuards: input is projected onto the lane axis and clamped to the strip, so line steps happen only by design (AI mistakes) or at lane ends when a human overshoots.\nAttackers collide with guards and each other using soft circle separation (maximum correction per tick) so nobody is trapped; teammates in lanes never block each other.\nCourt edge: attackers crossing a sideline or end line outside the allowed return are tagged by rule (D15).\n22.5 Tag validator\nImplements Section 9.2 exactly: eligibility, foot-circle position test, reach-segment versus attacker capsule overlap during the active ticks, hold window, deterministic tie-break (earlier tick, then smaller distance, then lower seat). Output is an event with a reason code that the HUD, officials, audio and replay all consume.\n22.6 AI implementation\nPerception: each AI reads a delayed snapshot (ring buffer of recent states) of the tier’s reaction ticks; it never reads current hidden state.\nGuard brain: predicts each attacker’s crossing point with linear extrapolation over the tier’s horizon, moves along the lane to cover the nearest predicted crossing, starts a tag when the predicted attacker enters reach at the active window, and keeps a margin from line zones (smaller margin and random slips on lower tiers).\nAttacker brain: for each row it scores candidate crossing points by distance from the nearest guard’s reach envelope plus the guard’s turning cost, commits to the best gap, sprints when the gap is open and stamina allows, and aborts or feints when the guard commits early (feints only in Pro).\nCoordinator: staggers entry times and decoys, assigns Sikka and lanes when the captain is AI, and reassigns when a lane is vacated.\nSoak test: thousands of headless AI-vs-AI matches per format must stay in the 30–45% round-trip band and produce zero illegal AI moves.\n22.7 Rendering\nProcedural athletes from capsule, sphere and box primitives with a skeleton of nested groups; procedural idle, walk, run, sprint, tag reach, stumble, celebrate animations driven by speed; kits, skin tone, hair and armband applied as colours and simple canvas textures.\nCourt as flat planes: earth texture generated on a canvas, worn-grass patches, chalk lines drawn from the court data (so lines can never disagree with rules).\nMaps differ by props, palette and lighting only (Section 24). Crowd uses instanced low-poly figures on Balanced and High.\nCamera rigs per Section 11 with per-format presets, smoothing and ground/edge avoidance.\n22.8 UI, input, audio, localisation\nDOM overlay for HUD, menus and calls; touch controls use pointer events with a movable joystick and large tag and sprint buttons; keyboard and gamepad via the browser Gamepad API.\ni18n: all strings in bn.json and en.json, no text in textures; Bangla uses the system Bengali font via a safe font stack.\nAudio: oscillator and noise synthesis for whistle, footsteps by surface, tag slap, void-tag buzz, crowd bed, UI blips; a master volume and category sliders; all cues also appear visually.\n22.9 Replays and persistence\nA replay file is the profile id and version, format, map, seats and AI tiers, seed, and the per-tick input stream, compressed to a few KB per minute. Replaying re-simulates the match. Settings, stats, XP and unlocks are stored in localStorage with a schema version and a safe reset.\n22.10 Online design (v1.1)\nNode ws server runs the same rules and simulation modules at 30 Hz; clients send inputs with sequence numbers and run client-side prediction at 60 Hz.\nSnapshots at 15 Hz with about 100 ms interpolation; reconciliation snaps or smooths without altering rule events.\nLag compensation for tags is capped at 120 ms; lobbies fill unfilled seats with AI; disconnect handling per Section 14.\n23.
